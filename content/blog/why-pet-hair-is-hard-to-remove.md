Anyone who drives with a dog knows the routine. You vacuum the cargo area, it looks better, and then you notice the carpet is still covered in hair that the vacuum never touched.

That is not a sign you are doing it wrong. Pet hair in a car is genuinely one of the harder cleaning problems, and it is worth knowing why.

## The hair works its way into the fibers

Car carpet and cloth upholstery are made of tight, short fibers. Pet hair, especially the short, coarse kind, gets pushed into those fibers every time someone sits, steps or a dog shifts around on the seat. Over time it weaves in rather than sitting on top.

A vacuum is good at picking up what is loose. It is not good at pulling out hair that is anchored in the material. That is why the surface can look clean while the carpet still feels fuzzy when you run a hand over it.

## It collects in places you do not see

Seat rails, the gaps between seats and the console, under the seats, seat belt tracks and cargo area trim all catch hair. Hair also mixes with dirt and moisture, which can hold odors in the cabin longer than you would expect.

## Why heavy pet hair is its own category

Removing embedded pet hair is mostly a time problem. It has to be loosened and pulled out of the material section by section before the rest of the interior cleaning can really happen.

That is why we treat heavy pet hair as a different scope of work. A normal detail is built around normal use. When hair is embedded throughout the seats, carpet and cargo area, our Factory Reset is the service that gives the job the time it actually needs.

## Making it easier between visits

Seat covers and cargo liners catch a lot of hair before it reaches the carpet, and they are much easier to clean. Brushing a dog before a car ride helps too. Once the interior is fully reset, regular maintenance keeps hair from building back up to the same level.
