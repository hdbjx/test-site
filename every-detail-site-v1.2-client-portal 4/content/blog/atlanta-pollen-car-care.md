When pollen covers a car, the temptation is to wipe it off dry. That is usually the wrong move. The paint is covered in loose contamination, and dragging a dry towel across it can move that material over the finish instead of safely carrying it away.

The safer approach is to use water and proper wash lubrication so the contamination can be loosened and removed rather than rubbed around.

## Do not chase perfect every morning

During a heavy pollen stretch, a freshly cleaned car can look dusty again almost immediately. Constant dry wiping creates more contact with the paint without solving the source of the problem.

It is usually better to let a light layer sit until you can wash the vehicle properly, especially if the alternative is repeatedly rubbing a towel over a dry surface.

## A good wash process matters more than an aggressive product

Pollen itself does not require a complicated detailing chemical. A careful hand wash, clean wash media and proper drying technique are the important parts.

If the car is already on a regular cleaning schedule, pollen season is also a good example of why maintenance visits work. The vehicle can be cleaned before road film, interior dirt and seasonal contamination all stack up at once.

## Protection helps with maintenance, not immunity

Paint protection can make regular washing easier, but it does not create a force field that keeps pollen from landing on the car. Wax, sealant and ceramic coatings still need to be washed and maintained.

Think of protection as making the surface easier to care for, not eliminating the need to care for it.
